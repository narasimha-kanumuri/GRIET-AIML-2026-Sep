# Step 7 — Draw the Answer

> ## **KEY: Visualisation = Picture of the Answer**

---

## The 3-second test

Look at these seven numbers:

```
95   120   80   140   60   170   110
```

Which is the largest? Time yourself.

Now imagine a bar chart of the same numbers.
You would know in under a second, without reading a single digit.

**Nothing changed except the format.**

That is the entire argument for visualisation.

---

## Why this matters beyond class

You will one day hand your analysis to someone who:

- has four minutes
- will not read 30 rows
- will not read your code
- has to make a decision

They will look at one picture and decide.

Your chart *is* your report.

## Part B — Chart 1: Screen time by day

### English
> Draw a bar chart where each bar is a day
> and the height of the bar is the total screen time for that day.

Then:

### English
> Give the chart a title: "My Screen Time by Day".
> Label the bottom axis "Day".
> Label the side axis "Minutes".
> Show the chart.

**A chart with no title and no labels is not a finding. It is decoration.**

### Answer this from the picture alone
- Which day was your worst?
- Is there any pattern — weekends, exam days, anything?

---

## Part C — Chart 2: Where does the time go?

### English
> Calculate the total minutes for each of the four apps.
> Draw a bar chart comparing the four apps.
> Title it "Total Time by App".

### Answer this
- Which app quietly took the most time?
- Was it the app you expected?

---

## Part D — Chart 3: Study versus screen

### English
> Draw a chart with two lines:
> one for study minutes per day, one for total screen time per day.
> Add a legend so a reader knows which line is which.

### Answer this
- Do the lines move together, or in opposite directions?
- On your best study day, what happened to screen time?

---

## Part E — Optional: the share of attention

### English
> Draw a pie chart showing what share of your total app time each app took.

**Use a pie chart only when the parts add up to one whole.**
Do not use a pie chart for things that are not shares.

---

## Chart rules that make you look professional

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Always title the chart | Otherwise the reader guesses |
| Always label both axes | "120" of what? |
| Add a legend when there is more than one series | Otherwise lines are meaningless |
| Never use more colours than you need | Colour should mean something |
| One chart, one message | If it needs a paragraph, split it |

---

## The habit to carry forward

Every single time you produce a chart, finish the sentence:

> **"This chart shows that ______________, which means I should ______________."**

If you cannot finish that sentence, the chart is not done.
